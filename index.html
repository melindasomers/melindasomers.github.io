About Me

Hi, welcome to my site! My name is Melinda and I hail from northern New Jersey. I attended college at UNC Chapel Hill, where I earned my Bachelor's in Neuroscience. I currently work with Dr. Abigail Marsh as the lab manager in her Lab on Social & Affective Neuroscience at Georgetown University, studying individual differences in prosocial behavior. Specifically, is there something about people's intentions to act prosocially that determines whether they follow through on those behaviors? Building upon my experience at Georgetown, I am applying to Social Psychology PhD programs where I hope to further explore questions related to prosociality, social connection, and wellbeing.

During my time as an undergraduate at UNC, I was fortunate to gain a breadth of research experiences working with several PIs who I am grateful to call my mentors. I started as research assistant with Dr. Kim Ritola helping to launch her viral vector core in the UNC Neuroscience Center. Here, I built my wet lab skills and even worked with rodent models alongside one of our collaborators to see how our viruses were used in action! Hoping to dive deeper into working with human research subjects, I connected with Dr. Gabriella Alvarez, who at the time was a graduate student mentored by Dr. Keely Muscatell, Assistant Professor in the Psychology & Neuroscience Department. Under their mentorship, I helped to launch and collect data for a placebo-controlled, within-subjects, crossover pharmacological intervention and neuroimaging study. We recruited adults facing chronic stress due to long-term unemployment and targeted the inflammation in their brain (with minocycline) to investigate effects on neural reward pathways. For my senior thesis, I accumulated daily survey data from these participants to compare within-subjects differences of placebo vs. active minocycline on their daily perceived stress. We found that attenuating neuroinflammation with minocycline has the potential to modulate perceived stress in a chronically stressed population.

I am passionate about research that can be applied outside of the lab. As a yoga instructor in my free time, I cultivate an environment based on collective breath, movement, and social connection.
